# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

Open Prep is for learners, educators, coaches, firms, content contributors, and maintainers with different backgrounds, identities, abilities, and levels of consulting experience. We are committed to a welcoming, accessible, and constructive community where everyone can participate without harassment, abuse, discrimination, or pressure to disclose personal information.

## Expected Behavior

Community members are expected to:

- Treat others with respect, patience, and empathy, including when giving or receiving feedback.
- Welcome different perspectives, experience levels, communication styles, and accessibility needs.
- Discuss ideas and contributions rather than attacking or dismissing people.
- Accept responsibility for mistakes, correct harm where possible, and respect reasonable boundaries.
- Protect other people's privacy and obtain consent before sharing private messages, personal information, learner work, or conduct reports.
- Disclose relevant personal, professional, institutional, or financial conflicts when reviewing or moderating contributions.
- Use project spaces only for good-faith work related to consulting preparation and the Open Prep community.

## Unacceptable Behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, stalking, unwanted sexual attention, or sustained disruption.
- Hateful, abusive, dehumanizing, or discriminatory language or conduct based on identity, background, health, disability, age, experience, or another personal characteristic.
- Personal attacks, humiliation, deliberate misgendering, or targeted exclusion.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private or sensitive information without permission.
- Pressuring anyone to reveal personal, educational, employment, health, or demographic information that is not needed for project participation.
- Retaliating against someone for raising a concern, providing evidence, participating in a review, or recusing from a decision.
- Abusing a maintainer, reviewer, educator, employer, or other position of authority.
- Knowingly making a false report to harm another person. A good-faith report is not a violation merely because it cannot be substantiated.

## Scope

This Code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, community content work, project events, and other communication or collaboration spaces managed by Open Prep. It also applies when someone officially represents the project, such as by using an official project account or acting in an assigned maintainer role.

## Private Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately through the repository's **Security** tab using **Report a vulnerability**. Begin the report title with `Conduct report:` so maintainers can route it appropriately. Using this private intake for a conduct concern does not classify the report as a security vulnerability; it is only the repository's private reporting channel. Do not put sensitive details in a public issue, pull request, or discussion. Share only the information reasonably needed to understand the concern, such as what happened, where and when it occurred, who was involved, and any relevant private links or records.

If private repository reporting is unavailable, contact a repository maintainer through a private contact route listed on that maintainer's GitHub profile. If the report concerns a maintainer, identify the conflict and request review by a different, unconflicted maintainer. Actual security vulnerabilities remain governed by and should be reported as described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md); security reports and conduct reports follow separate review paths.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will handle reports in good faith and as promptly as practical. Depending on the circumstances, they will:

1. Confirm receipt and assess immediate safety, privacy, and conflict-of-interest concerns.
2. Gather only the information needed for a fair review and, when safe and appropriate, give affected people an opportunity to respond.
3. Decide whether this Code was violated and choose a proportionate response.
4. Communicate the outcome to the reporter and the person affected by the decision as appropriate, while protecting confidential information.
5. Review restrictions when new evidence, an appeal, or a stated review date warrants it.

Responses may include a private clarification, a request to stop or correct behavior, a warning, removal of content, temporary participation limits, removal from a project role, or a permanent ban. Maintainers may take temporary protective action while a report is reviewed. Enforcement decisions should consider severity, impact, context, prior behavior, willingness to repair harm, and the safety of the community.

## Confidentiality And Conflicts

Reports, evidence, and identities will be shared only with people who need them to review the concern or protect participants. Maintainers will avoid public details unless disclosure is necessary for safety, required by law, or authorized by the affected person. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised, but unnecessary collection, sharing, and retention of sensitive information will be avoided.

A maintainer or reviewer with a personal, professional, institutional, financial, or other material conflict must disclose it and recuse from the decision. The report must then be handled by an unconflicted maintainer or reviewer.

## Appeal And Review

A person directly affected by an enforcement decision may request a private review through the same repository Security intake using a title that begins `Conduct report: appeal`. If private repository reporting is unavailable, use the fallback route above. The appeal should identify the decision and briefly explain any procedural concern, relevant new evidence, or reason the response was disproportionate. An unconflicted reviewer who did not make the original decision will review the available record where practical and communicate the result privately. Retaliation for requesting a good-faith review is prohibited.
